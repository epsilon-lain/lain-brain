## Project entry

**Lain ZIP - Checkable and Revisable Memory for Apertus**  
Independent research prototype, October 2026. Project direction and experiment design by epsilon-lain; implementation and documentation developed with AI assistance.

I investigate how language-model agents can maintain external knowledge records whose scope, provenance and revisions are inspectable. I developed experiments using Apertus, versioned memory, exact polynomial verification and raw-before-grade evidence storage. A frozen 70B pilot demonstrated a limited inherited-memory repair workflow: three of four wrong-rule episodes met a strict two-follow-up criterion, with no mistaken withdrawal in two correct-note controls. Revised notes removed two observed poisoned-memory failures on a repeated task but did not exceed the no-memory content baseline. I also investigated outcome-trained memory control; 128 real external-controller updates exposed collapse toward abstention rather than useful conditional repair.

This work taught me to distinguish a system that can execute a repair from a policy that learns when to repair. I am interested in research training that would help me design stronger objectives, independent evaluations and comparisons under limited resources. The project is a research prototype with reproducible evidence, not a peer-reviewed publication or proof of general autonomous learning.
